# saved-spec companion handoff

use when the active agent router supplies a visual-companion skill during an
authorized saved material spec task. keep the Markdown spec canonical and the
change atlas as its full review projection. the companion answers a bounded
question such as “which record belongs where?” or “what changes at this step?”

## trigger and fit

read the available companion skill's entry point; resolve it from the active
skill catalog, never from a hardcoded home path. run this pass automatically
after drafting or materially revising the saved spec, before requesting its
acceptance. creating the companion is covered by the authorized spec task;
it does not require another invocation or permission prompt.

choose the smallest supported instrument that adds explanatory value:

- related records plus a supported append-record event: source-linked anatomy and authored trace, with a valid and a boundary case.
- dense phases or capability dispositions: the review instrument, preserving every required outcome, dependency, gate, uncertainty, and guardrail.
- unsupported behavior or an already-clear small transition: keep the atlas and explain briefly why no companion was added. do not invent records, business rules, or an event just to fit a template.

the owner's text-only request skips generated visual surfaces. discussion,
unsaved brainstorming, Resume, and Audit stay read-only unless separately
authorized. if the companion skill or its required local runtime is absent,
finish with the atlas and disclose that limitation; do not install dependencies,
call providers, or turn a successful spec draft into a blocked task.

## construct and bind

1. state the concrete question, included material, and omissions. keep technical depth; put help at the missing connection rather than assuming a beginner audience.
2. author input from the exact saved spec and any necessary cited sources. preserve identifiers, excerpt wording, qualifications, evidence status, and unresolved conflicts. a proposed rule can be `draft` while its example trace is `illustrative`.
3. use each cited document's repository-relative label/locator and full content fingerprint as its revision marker. show that provenance in the companion. for review formats without a dedicated revision field, include the fingerprint in the canonical-source display label.
4. keep authored input and the generated bundle in an explicit project-owned or ignored local destination, separate from the atlas. follow the companion skill's builder, empty-destination, plain-text rendering, and offline requirements. no project text belongs in either reusable skill package.
5. verify source/object correspondence, displayed counts and identifiers, trace outcomes or review gates, keyboard behavior, and available rendered checks. report missing browser proof instead of substituting syntax checks for visual comfort.

the companion is authored from a bounded interpretation; it is not the atlas's
deterministic extraction of the complete spec. on any cited-source change,
recheck the excerpts, model, and fingerprint, then rebuild into a fresh
destination. never just replace the fingerprint on stale content. the atlas's
`--check` does not validate a companion; do not present an older companion as
current while accepting a newer spec revision.

## present and preserve authority

link the companion, atlas, and canonical spec together in the handoff, with
one short sentence naming the companion's question and scope. keep source
fidelity, interaction checks, rendered inspection, and owner understanding
distinct. selection, traversal, and review marks accept nothing and edit no
source. owner acceptance applies only to the exact canonical spec revision.
